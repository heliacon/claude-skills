# Marketing context doc

One file every marketing task reads first. Keep it short and factual. Each claim names its source
(an interview, a review, a support ticket, an analytics query) and a date, so stale facts are visible.

## Template

```markdown
# Marketing context (last reviewed: YYYY-MM-DD)

## Product in one line
What it does, for whom, in the customer's words.

## Ideal customer
- Segment:
- Trigger that starts their search:
- What they use today (competitive alternative):
- Why our unique attributes matter to them:

## Anti-personas
Who looks like a fit but is not, and why (churns, costs too much to serve, wants something else).

## Positioning statement
Alternatives / unique attributes / value / segment / category / trend.

## Switching forces
- Push (pain with the current way):
- Pull (what draws them to us):
- Habit (what keeps them where they are):
- Anxiety (what worries them about switching):

## Objections and answers
| Objection | How often heard | Answer | Proof |

## Customer language
Verbatim phrases from interviews, reviews and support. Quote, do not paraphrase.

## Proof points
| Claim | Evidence | Source | Date | Public? |

## Primary motion, wedge and channels
Motion:  Wedge use case:  Channels (owned / rented / borrowed):

## Open questions
What we do not know yet and how we will find out.
```

## Filling it

Ask the person who owns the product before guessing. Useful questions:

- Who bought most recently, and what happened the week before they started looking?
- What were they using before? What did they say was wrong with it?
- Who signed up and left quickly? What did they expect that we did not do?
- What do customers call the product when they describe it to a colleague?
- Which claim do prospects doubt most? What would convince them?
- What evidence exists today that we could publish (benchmarks, audits, numbers we may share)?

Mine existing material for customer language: support tickets, sales call notes, review sites,
community threads, churn survey answers. Copy the phrases exactly.

Habit and anxiety are the forces teams forget. A strong pull loses to a familiar workaround plus fear
of a painful migration. If either is high, the message must reduce it (import tools, a free
migration, a reversible trial) rather than shout louder about the pull.
