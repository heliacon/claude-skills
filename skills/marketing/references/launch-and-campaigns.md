# Launches and campaigns

## Launch readiness gate

A launch does not go ahead until every line has evidence. A missing line moves the date, not the bar.

- [ ] The product does what the launch says, verified by someone outside the team on a clean account
- [ ] Activation path works end to end: sign-up, first success, a way to get help
- [ ] Proof asset is ready and public: demo, benchmark, write-up or customer result
- [ ] Pricing page and billing work, if the launch sells anything
- [ ] Owned channels updated first: site, docs, changelog, email list
- [ ] Borrowed and rented channels booked with dates and owners
- [ ] Someone watches support and community channels for the first 48 hours
- [ ] Success metric, baseline and review date written down
- [ ] Follow-up touches planned for the weeks after (not only launch day)

Size the launch to the change. A minor feature gets a changelog entry and an email line. A new
product or a major capability gets the full sequence. Several smaller launches across a quarter
usually beat one large one, because each one is another reason to talk to the audience.

## Campaign brief template

Write this before any spend. Review it with whoever owns the budget.

```markdown
# Campaign: <name>

Goal:            One outcome, as a number with a date (e.g. 40 activated trials by 30 June).
Audience:        Segment from the context doc. Who is excluded.
Message:         The pillar this campaign carries, and its proof.
Offer:           What the audience gets for acting.
Channels:        Each one labelled owned / rented / borrowed, with owner.
Budget:          Total, split by channel, with a reason for each split.
Timeline:        Start, checkpoints, end.
Success metric:  Primary metric and baseline. Guardrails (for example cost per activation ceiling).
Kill criteria:   The result at the first checkpoint that stops or reallocates spend.
Risks:           What could go wrong (channel fails, message misreads, capacity) and the response.
Measurement:     How results are attributed, and the triangulation used.
```

Budget split default for an unproven channel mix: put most of the spend into the channel with
evidence, and a small fixed share into tests of new channels, each with its own kill criteria.
Reallocate at checkpoints on activation and cost per activation, not on clicks.
